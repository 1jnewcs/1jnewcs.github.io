Reading Water & Presentation

You can have the wrong fly and still catch fish if your presentation is perfect. The reverse is rarely true.

Current Seams

  • What it is: The edge where fast water meets slow water.
  • Why: Trout sit in the slow water, facing into the fast water, waiting for food to sweep by. Fish the seam, not the fast water.
  • (Watch Water Reading Videos)

Depth Control

  • The Rule: Nymphs should be ticking the bottom. If you aren't snagging occasionally, you aren't deep enough.
  • How: Add split shot, use a heavier bead head, or lengthen your leader between the indicator and the fly.
  • (Watch Depth Control Videos)

Drag-Free Drift

  • What it is: Your fly moving at the exact same speed as the current.
  • Why: If your line drags the fly faster or slower than the natural food, fish will refuse it.
  • Fix: Mend your line immediately after casting to reposition the fly line upstream of the fly.
  • (Watch Mending Videos)

Fly Tying Rules of Thumb

Tying flies is an art, but there are fundamental rules that make the process easier and the flies more effective.

Proportions

  • The Rule: The hook shank is your ruler.
  • Dry Fly: Tail should be roughly the length of the hook shank. Hackle should reach just past the hook point.
  • Nymph: Body should taper. Thorax should be thicker than the abdomen.
  • (Watch Proportion Videos)

Less is More

  • The Rule: When in doubt, use less dubbing and fewer wraps of hackle.
  • Why: Overdressed flies look unnatural, don't sink or float as well, and are harder to cast.
  • (Watch Dubbing Videos)

Thread Tension

  • The Rule: You shouldn't break thread while tying.
  • Why: You need enough tension to secure materials, but not so much that you crush the hook shank or break the thread. Let the bobbin hang to check tension.
  • (Watch Thread Tension Videos)

Trout Behavior & Feeding

Understanding where trout live and how they eat is more important than the fly you tie on.

Where They Hold

  • Cover: Trout seek protection from predators and fast currents. Look for undercut banks, log jams, large rocks, and deep pools.
  • Oxygen: Fast, choppy water holds more oxygen. Trout will move into riffles to breathe.
  • (Watch Trout Holding Videos)

How They See

  • Vision: Trout have a wide field of vision, especially upward. They can see you if you cast a shadow or stand too close to the bank.
  • Approach: Always approach the water from the side or behind. Keep a low profile.
  • (Watch Trout Vision Videos)

Hatches & Timing

Knowing when bugs hatch is the key to matching the hatch.

Time of Day

  • Morning/Evening: Most mayfly and caddis hatches occur in the low light of morning or evening.
  • Midday: Midges and terrestrials (ants, beetles) are active midday.
  • (Watch Hatch Timing Videos)

Time of Year

  • Spring: Blue-Winged Olives (BWOs), March Browns, Caddis.
  • Summer: PMDs, Tricos (late summer), Terrestrials (hoppers, ants, beetles).
  • Fall: BWOs, October Caddis, Streamers.
  • Winter: Midges. Fish deep and slow.
  • (Watch Hatch Chart Videos)

Fly Selection Logic

Choosing the right fly is a process of elimination.

Match the Hatch vs. Attractor

  • Match the Hatch: When fish are actively feeding on a specific insect, match the size, shape, and color exactly.
  • Attractor: When no fish are rising, use a fly that triggers a predatory response (e.g., flashy nymphs, streamers, or terrestrials).
  • (Watch Fly Selection Videos)

Size Matters Most

  • The Rule: If you can't match the exact color or shape, match the size first. A size 16 olive fly will catch more fish than a size 10 brown fly during a size 16 BWO hatch.
  • (Watch Size Selection Videos)

Rigging & Indicators

How you set up your rig determines how quickly you detect strikes.

Strike Detection

  • Indicators: Use a yarn, foam, or float indicator. Position it so the flies drift at the right depth.
  • Tightline: In Euro nymphing, you don't use an indicator. You watch the sighter (a brightly colored section of leader) for any unnatural movement.
  • (Watch Indicator Setup Videos)

Safety & Ethics

Respect the resource and the fish.

Barbless Hooks

  • Why: Barbless hooks are easier to remove from a fish's mouth, reducing stress and mortality. They also come out of your clothing easier!
  • How: Pinch the barb down with pliers before tying on the fly.
  • (Watch Barbless Hook Videos)

Catch and Release

  • The Rule: Keep the fish in the water as much as possible. Use a rubber net. Handle with wet hands. Don't squeeze the fish.
  • (Watch Catch & Release Videos)
